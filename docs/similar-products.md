# Similar Products — AI in Higher Education

A researched landscape of existing products that cover **AI in higher education** — newsletters, blogs, podcasts, and organizational publications — ranked by audience size. This is the competitive field HAIgher Education enters.

Every link below was checked and resolves to a live, current page (see [Link verification](#link-verification)).

## The short answer: who has the biggest audience?

- **Biggest overall reach:** [**Inside Higher Ed**](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence) — ~2.8M monthly site visitors — but AI is only one section of a broad higher-ed news site, not its whole audience.
- **Biggest *AI-focused* voice in academia:** [**One Useful Thing** by Ethan Mollick](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/) — ~460K+ subscribers. Not higher-ed-specific, but the single most influential newsletter on AI in academic life, and the reach benchmark everyone else is measured against.
- **Biggest newsletter built *specifically* around AI in education:** [**Dr Phil's Newsletter** by Philippa Hardman](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/) — ~30K subscribers (leans learning-design / L&D).
- **General-AI scale benchmark:** [**The Neuron**](https://www.theneurondaily.com/) — 700K+ readers — included only to show what a mass-market *general* AI newsletter looks like; education is not its focus.

**No existing product does what HAIgher Education proposes** — continuous, multi-channel, multilingual collection structured into a knowledge graph. The field is made of individual expert newsletters and broad trade-news sites, not intelligence engines.

## How to read the audience numbers

**Treat every number as an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a measurement.**

- Most newsletters here run on Substack or beehiiv and **do not publish hard subscriber counts.** The figures come from self-reported "X subscribers" labels, leaderboard ranks, or third-party estimates surfaced in search — all of which round and drift over time.
- The org outlets (Inside Higher Ed, Chronicle, EDUCAUSE) report **whole-site or whole-membership** figures, not AI-specific audiences, so they are *not* directly comparable to a single newsletter's subscriber count.
- Some authors' real reach is on **LinkedIn**, not their newsletter (e.g. Jason Gulya is ~2–3K on Substack but ~30K on LinkedIn), so a Substack number can understate true influence.
- Ranks 2–7 in the core list are close enough that small changes could reorder them.

Where a number is genuinely unknown, the table says so rather than guessing.

## What this means for HAIgher Education

- **The category is crowded with individual expert voices, not systems.** Nearly every competitor is one person's newsletter reflecting their perspective. HAIgher Education's differentiator is being a *comprehensive, structured* source rather than another opinion feed.
- **The biggest audiences are adjacent, not head-on.** The mass reach (Inside Higher Ed, The Neuron, Mollick) sits either broader or narrower than "AI + universities" specifically — suggesting room for a dedicated, complete source, but also that attention is already concentrated in a few large players.
- **Several competitors already publish knowledge-graph-like artifacts** (Lance Eaton's crowdsourced AI-syllabus-policy database; EDUCAUSE's annual AI Landscape Study). These are both competition and potential data sources.
